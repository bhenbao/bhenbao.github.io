Antenna Deployer Module

UBC Orbit · Mechanical Design Developer

As part of UBC Orbit, a student CubeSat design team, I lead the structural design iterations for the antenna deployer module for our 1U satellite, ALEASAT which is planned to launch in 2028. The module stores and deploys the satellite's antennas after reaching orbit. My work spans mechanical design, manufacturing, testing, and integration with the electrical and controls teams.

How it works

The CubeSat must remain within the dimensional constraints of its launch container during launch, which limits how far components can protrude from the spacecraft. The antennas therefore need to be stowed inside the satellite during launch and deployed once in orbit. The module stores the antennas in a coiled configuration and releases them after deployment. The antennas are made from tape-measure material, which naturally returns toward its extended shape. A Dyneema line holdsthe deployer door closed, and the door in turn restrains the coiled antennas. To deploy the antennas, current heats resistors against the line until it burns through, releasing the door and allowing the antennas to extend.

Antenna Testing

Because the antennas are stored under significant curvature, I am testing their plastic deformation to determine how tightly they could be coiled without exceeding the maximum deployment deflection. I am testing antenna samples across six different coil radii, measuring their resulting deformation to identify a radius that balances compact packaging with the ability to send and recieve signals.

Environmental Testing

For our recent environmental test campaign at ESA test facilities, I helped to assmeble the ADM, as well as other satellite components under clearnroom conditions. The module underwent vibration and thermal vacuum (TVAC) testing. During TVAC testing, the antennas failed to deploy, likely due to increased friction from TVAC conditions, and the module is currently being redeisgned.
