Antenna Deployer Module

UBC Orbit · Mechanical Design Developer

As a part of UBC Orbit, a student CubeSat design team, I lead the structural design of the antenna deployer module for our 1U satellite, which is planned to launch in 2028. I'm in my second year on the team. My work includes custom designing a burn-wire door release mechanism, integrating electrical components, and conducting tests.

How it works

A 1U CubeSat is only 10 cm on each side, so there's little room for antennas at full length. The module stows the antennas coiled, then releases them once the satellite is in orbit. They are made from tape-measure material, which springs back to its extended shape. A Dyneema line holds the door closed, and the door in turn restrains the antennas. To deploy, current heats resistors under the line until it burns through. The door is released, and the antennas extend. Burn-wire release is a common choice on small satellites because it's compact and low cost.

Design and testing

The structure is 6061-T6 aluminum with a Type III hard-coat anodized finish. The module has been through vibration and thermal vacuum (TVAC) testing at ESA test facilities. During the design phase, I tested the plastic deformation of the antennas to find the optimal coiling radius for the module.
